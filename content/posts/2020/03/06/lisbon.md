Upon landing in Lisbon and getting a Viva Viagem card for public transit, I took the metro to the apartment I'd be staying at for the next month. My primary goal for my stay was to learn how to make authentic pastéis de nata, a type of Portuguese egg custard tart. When I got to the apartment, I was a little disappointed to find that the "well equipped" kitchen didn't even have a measuring cup—just a couple pots and pans and a faint odor of fish coming from the empty fridge.

![The small apartment in Alfama that I stayed in](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-1627077950.jpg)

After checking out the kitchen, I promptly found the bedroom and fell asleep despite it being nine o clock in the morning—I hadn't managed to sleep on the overnight flight as usual. When I woke up in the afternoon the realization that I was in Lisbon finally hit me—I didn't know the city, I didn't know the customs, and I didn't know the language. The anxiety set in—this was real and I didn't know anything about this place.

The next day I distracted myself with work to deal with the anxiety. That night around 8:30 pm I forced myself to finally leave the apartment to go out to a nearby restaurant to eat. I didn't know it at the time, but 8-9 is prime time for dinner in Portugal, so the place was packed. Being by myself, I was seated quickly, although it was at the end of a larger table that was already occupied. The waiter that served me was a grumpy looking gray-haired man who didn't speak English. I managed to order, but the whole situation caused my anxiety to spike. The restaurant was a traditional Portuguese place, and the food was filling but nothing special in my opinion. After taking the dishes away, I expected my check to come, but it never did. Not sure what to do and overcome with anxiety, I sat there for almost half an hour. Eventually I flagged him down and left, but the experience scarred me. Despite not having any food in my apartment, I didn't leave it for the next two days.

![O Pitéu da Graça, a Portuguese restaurant better suited for locals](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-5393083251.jpg)

![Panoramic view on my walk back from the restaurant](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/pano_20200129_215511.vr_.jpg)

Lisbon wasn't actually my first choice for first places to go. It was a last minute change from Australia because of the fires that were raging at the time. If it wasn't a last minute change, I might have prepared a little better for basic Portuguese language and culture. I hoped it wouldn't matter much because from what I'd heard English proficiency was really high in Lisbon, and it has the largest digital nomad presence in Europe.

I was determined to go to the largest of the digital nomad meetups that Thursday night. It was a 30 minute walk in the rain from where I was staying, but I knew I needed to leave the apartment if I was ever going to feel comfortable in Lisbon. Since the meetup was geared towards nomads and expats, the lingua franca was English, so at least I wouldn't have to worry about that. I got there just as the event was starting, so the place was still a little empty and I wasn't so sure I was in the right place. 

![Le Chat after a few more people started showing up](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/83927593_10156537601901572_2930370707621675008_n.jpg)

I stuck around, and as the night continued, the bar filled up. Others who had been to previous meetups said this was actually a low turnout because of the rain. Many of the people I talked to had traveled for a while and decided on Lisbon as their place to set up base. I even ran into a woman from Iowa who had been living in Lisbon for the past 8 years! I didn't meet many programmers, but I did meet one guy named Nick who was also in web development. Nick and I started talking to an Australian couple, Arlindo and Nicola, who had recently moved to Lisbon permanently and were starting a fitness class in the city.

Arlindo was originally from Portugal, but only recently had moved back. His class was a ground based bodyweight exercise called Animal Flow. It seemed to me to be a bit like yoga, but with a lot more movement and an almost dance-like quality. I figured joining a fitness class would be good for me, so Nick and I agreed to join the class the following Sunday afternoon.

![My third animal flow class](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/flow64-2.gif)

![Madame Petisca, a rooftop bar we'd go to after class](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-817125362.jpg)

The class was held in Mendia Palace which was a beautiful old building originally built in the 17th century. The palace was run down these days, but was going to be renovated into a hotel after being purchased for 13 million euros by Selina. In the meantime it was being used for fitness classes. I really enjoyed the class—it was something really different from what I had done before, and I decided I would be continuing with classes every Sunday.

The next week I was starting to feel more comfortable, so I began my search for pastel de nata supplies. Google maps showed a single kitchen store nearby me, so on Monday I decided to walk around some over lunch and buy the cupcake tin and some measuring utensils. When I got to the store, nobody was there. It was closed. A little discouraged, I decided to walk around a bit to see if there was another place that might sell some. I walked around a bit more before heading back to the apartment, empty handed. On my way back in a narrow side street I saw some kitchen supplies filling the entirety of a tiny shop in a narrow side street. I showed the cashier a photo of what I was looking for and he dug through the pile of things and pulled out a cupcake tin. Finally some success! Unfortunately, he didn't have any measuring utensils either. Maybe I could just eyeball it?

![Some street art on a random street](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/img_20200223_173819.jpg)

The next thing I would need were the pastel de nata supplies. I had seen a store near my apartment that looked like it might have what I needed. I picked up some ovos, a limão, manteiga, leite meio gordo, and glúten do trigo. I still needed sugar, vanilla, and a cinnamon stick, but I was off to a good start. I had everything to make the pastry dough which would have to rest in the fridge overnight anyway. Because I still didn't have real food, I grabbed a frozen pizza from the convenience store outside my place and finished up my shopping for the day.

That night I threw the pizza in the oven and started making the dough. Upon mixing the water with the glúten de trigo, I realized that glúten de trigo didn't actually mean flour. It started clumping together into a sort of rubbery substance. I accidentally bought wheat *gluten*, not wheat *flour*. At least frozen pizzas are a lot harder to mess up.

![Praça Dom Pedro IV that I walked through rather frequently](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-1820993597.jpg)

![A park in Cais do Sodré where I would relax and watch the sailboats](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-1863642166.jpg)

The next day I searched Google maps for a proper supermarket. There was a health food store not too far away called Celeiro Dieta—I'd actually already walked past it on my way to the fitness class. And it looked like it had a restaurant underneath it too that I could eat lunch at. The lunch there was one of my favorites. It was a typical supermarket restaurant like you'd find in the US, but they served a lot of food and it only cost me like €5. I couldn't find regular flour—*farinha* de trigo for reference—at this store, but I did find gluten free flour, so I figured I might be able to mix the gluten with the gluten free flour and make something edible—later I learned that you can't. I also made my way to the kitchen store from earlier, which was now open, and I got my measuring cups and spoons.

That week I also had some travel plans change. My team was planning on going to Tokyo for an in-person meetup that I was really excited for. Japan is my favorite country that I've been to and it happened to coincide with Elm Japan, a tech conference that I would be able to go to. Unfortunately, amid the worsening COVID-19 condition in Asia, we decided to move the meetup to Lisbon instead. Kinda disappointing, but at least it would be really easy for me since I didn't have to travel anywhere for it. I booked a couple more places to stay, a 10 bed dorm in a hostel and a private room in a coliving space so I could try out some of the different accommodation types and see what I would like best.

![The requisite coffee shop + laptop photo](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/img_20200214_150321.jpg)

On the weekend, Nick invited me to join some of his friends to go to a rooftop bar in Barrio Alto called Park Bar. The view was pretty spectacular from the rooftop, overlooking the 25 de Abril Bridge, obviously named in honor of my birthday and not the Carnation Revolution in 1974. After some drinks, about half our group wanted to continue partying into the night, so we grabbed an Uber to Lux, the most famous nightclub in the city. We maybe got there around 2am, but there was still a line out the door to get in. After dancing for hours, some of us left between 6 and 7am and walked back to our places just in time for sunrise.

I slept until 3pm when I hurriedly got ready to go to Animal Flow at 4pm. I walked as quickly as I could, but got there too late and was locked out. It was probably for the best because I'm not too sure how well I would have done after the previous night. Instead I decided to try again at finding a proper supermarket.

![View of the April 25th Bridge from Park Bar](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-1300466164.jpg)

![View of the April 25th Bridge from Cais do Sodré](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-1779929451.jpg)

This time I found my way to Lidl which was a bit of a hilly walk from my apartment. It was well worth it because I *finally* found the groceries that I needed. I could also employ my favorite shopping technique of buying the things that were on sale and figuring out how to cook them later. I walked back with my bag almost overflowing and a green melon under my arm.

Since the apartment didn't have a baking sheet, I made chicken croissants instead of chicken strips in the cupcake tin, the only metal baking thing that I had. That, along with some potatoes made the first proper meal that I had cooked in Lisbon. I also managed to make a pretty tasty soup with the carrots and leeks that were on sale.

![Chicken croissants](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-1227775358.jpg)

![Carrot leek soup](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-116631972.jpg)

Now, with all the pastel de nata ingredients I could finally start! I wanted to make a first batch before trying some from a pasteleria here. They turned out pretty good—the bottoms were a little soggy, but they tasted phenomenal and they looked just like the pictures, so I was quite happy with a first try. I had nabbed some pastéis from Lidl as a comparison. Mine, being fresh out of the oven, were much crispier (at the top, where they cooked properly), but a little thicker than the store-bought kind. My custard was a tad more sweet, but was otherwise on the money.

![My first try at Pastéis de nata](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/img_20200221_151200.jpg)

![The original Pastéis de Belém](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-1841254658.jpg)

The next step would be to go to Belem, home to the original pasteis de nata. I caught the tram there the following weekend. Belem was beautiful, and it couldn't have been a more perfect day! I wandered around first for a bit doing the sightseeing. Eventually I made my way to the famous pasteleria. I was not disappointed. The pastry was flaky and delicious with the signature swirl on the bottom. The custard, sweet with a hint of cinnamon. I had made it close in my first attempt, but I knew what I had to adjust for the next round.

I finished out the day with some of the other touristy stuff. Walking down Rua Augusta to the Praça do Comércio and following the river to Cais do Sodré.

![The famous Torre de Belém](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/img_20200222_143444.jpg)

![Praça do Comércio, maybe second or third most touristy spot in Lisbon](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-975925046.jpg)

The next week was my last in the Graça apartment—my last chance to visit Castelo de São Jorge when I was so near and my last chance to use my kitchen. The castle had a wonderful view of the city, some peacocks, and a nice little exhibit explaining the history of the place. Back at my apartment I made two more batches and got as close as I was going to get. Flaky pastry, sweet cinnamon-y custard—I had done it.

![One of the many peacocks wandering around the castle](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-130909291.jpg)

![It was kind of rainy, but I still think it was worth the €10 to get in](https://ajlende.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/snapchat-1568302768.jpg)

With only three more weeks in Lisbon, I had to decide on a destination to go to next. With friends visiting Europe in the coming months and my sister also visiting for a bit, I needed to leave the Schengen area and save some of my visa time for them. London seemed like the ideal place to spend the next bit of time—maybe I'll stick to something easier than pastries there, like bangers and mash or something 🙃
